1. CENTRAL CHANGE OF CLAIM

The rejected strong claim was that performance functionals could be assigned to a small set of hard universality classes with fixed effective exponents, and that a fixed transport/direct fusion would transfer across task families.

The revised claim is deeper and narrower:

A. Universal objects are candidate error components, not fixed numerical exponents.
B. An observed finite-budget exponent is a local emergent quantity generated by a target-, metric-, evidence- and budget-dependent mixture.
C. Transport evidence is not permanently equivalent to a fixed number of labels. When it contains residual bias, it has a finite validity horizon.
D. Safe deployed-performance auditing therefore requires reliability gating, budget-adaptive evidence weighting, and an explicit no-harm fallback to direct evidence.

2. OBSERVABILITY AS AN EVIDENCE STATE

For target environment t, performance functional m, evidence channel e, target-label budget b, and decision tolerance epsilon, define the observability state

O(t,m,e,b,epsilon)

as the attainable uncertainty or risk of estimating the latent deployed performance theta_t,m under the allowed evidence protocol.

Performance observability is not an intrinsic scalar property of the source model. It is relational: it depends jointly on the target environment, functional geometry, evidence channel, budget and tolerated decision error.

3. COMPONENT-MIXTURE LAW

The finite-budget estimation error is represented as

E_t,m,e(b)
= c_0,t,m,e
+ c_1/2,t,m,e (b/b0)^(-1/2)
+ c_1,t,m,e (b/b0)^(-1),

where all coefficients are non-negative.

Interpretation:

c0: persistent floor, including transport bias, irreducible protocol mismatch, or misspecification that is not removed by additional target labels.

c1/2: regular first-order empirical-process variation. Sample-average functionals such as Brier score and log loss are expected to be dominated by this term under regularity.

c1: higher-order or near-degenerate finite-budget contribution. Ranking functionals may express this term when first-order variance is attenuated, but metric identity alone does not fix its magnitude.

The effective exponent is

alpha_eff(b)
=
[0.5 c1/2 (b/b0)^(-1/2) + c1 (b/b0)^(-1)]
/
[c0 + c1/2 (b/b0)^(-1/2) + c1 (b/b0)^(-1)].

Consequences:

1. alpha_eff is generally budget dependent.
2. alpha_eff lies between 0 and 1 for non-negative components.
3. alpha_eff approaches 0 when a persistent floor dominates.
4. alpha_eff approaches 1/2 in a regular first-order regime.
5. alpha_eff may exceed 1/2 over a finite range when the higher-order term is substantial.
6. The same metric may display different effective exponents across targets.
7. A fixed metric-specific exponent is therefore a special case, not the general law.

4. THEOREM: NON-UNIVERSAL EXPONENTS FROM UNIVERSAL COMPONENTS

Let E(b)=c0+c1/2 b^(-1/2)+c1 b^(-1), with non-negative coefficients and at least two non-zero components. Then log E(b) is not globally linear in log b, and its local negative slope alpha_eff(b) varies with b. Therefore a single fitted power exponent depends on the observed budget interval and component weights.

This theorem explains why the U3C AUC exponents varied substantially even though the same witness definition was used: the functional label “AUC” does not uniquely determine the component mixture.

5. EVIDENCE VALIDITY HORIZON

Let D_b be a consistent direct target-witness estimator and T a transport estimator with residual error delta_T=T-theta.

Consider static fusion

F_b(lambda)=(1-lambda)D_b+lambda T.

If lambda is fixed and positive and delta_T is non-zero, then as b increases and D_b converges to theta,

F_b(lambda)-theta -> lambda delta_T.

Thus static fusion has a non-vanishing error floor. Since the direct error converges toward zero, there exists a finite budget after which static fusion is worse than direct-only estimation.

Define the evidence validity horizon

b_exp
=
inf{b: Risk[F_b] >= Risk[D_b]},

with b_exp=infinity if transport remains beneficial over the allowed budget range.

This converts “transport helps or hurts” into a sequential question: until what budget does the evidence retain positive value?

6. OPTIMAL WEIGHT AND EVIDENCE EXPIRY

Let v_D(b) denote direct-estimator variance, r_T the transport mean-squared error, and c_DT their covariance. The risk-minimizing transport weight is

lambda*(b)
=
clip[
(v_D(b)-c_DT)
/
(v_D(b)+r_T-2c_DT),
0,1
].

Under ordinary conditions v_D(b) decreases with budget. If transport error does not vanish with b, lambda*(b) decreases and ultimately approaches zero. Transport is therefore a prior with an expiry horizon, not an immutable label-equivalent asset.

7. RELIABILITY-GATED SAFE SEQUENTIAL AUDIT

The proposed audit has five modules.

7.1 Direct evidence
Estimate target performance from the currently labeled witness and estimate direct uncertainty by bootstrap or an analytically valid functional-specific variance estimator.

7.2 Transport reliability
Represent transport evidence as a point estimate plus calibrated residual risk. Reliability calibration must be cross-fitted and must include support/extrapolation diagnostics.

7.3 Budget-adaptive shrinkage
Weight transport according to direct uncertainty, calibrated transport risk, target support, and direct-transport disagreement. Transport weight must decline as direct evidence becomes more precise.

7.4 No-harm gate
Use transport only when an upper-bound risk proxy for fusion is no larger than the allowed direct-only risk plus a preregistered tolerance. Otherwise return direct-only.

7.5 Sequential action
At each budget return one of:
transport-assisted estimate;
direct-only estimate;
collect more labels;
decision reached;
abstain because the evidence protocol remains insufficient.

8. DEVELOPMENT ALGORITHM

For every held target:

1. exclude its true outcome from transport reliability calibration;
2. calibrate transport optimism and residual scale on the remaining targets;
3. estimate target support using only label-free descriptors;
4. estimate direct uncertainty using the repeated-witness distribution as a bootstrap surrogate;
5. choose method hyperparameters by inner leave-one-target-out validation;
6. evaluate the held target once;
7. aggregate all six outer held-target results.

The U3C targets are revealed development data. Results from this procedure are not confirmatory and cannot substitute for a new reserve.

9. TESTABLE FUTURE PREDICTIONS

A future untouched reserve should test:

P1. Component-mixture predictions from budgets 8–32 outperform fixed hard-class exponents at budgets 64–128.

P2. Effective exponents vary within metric families and are explained by changing component fractions.

P3. Static positive transport weights develop an error floor whenever transport bias persists.

P4. Evidence-validity horizons can be predicted within a preregistered adjacent-budget tolerance.

P5. Reliability-gated adaptive auditing is non-inferior to direct-only overall, improves low-budget estimation in a majority of targets, and automatically withdraws transport as direct evidence accumulates.

11. NATURE-LEVEL POSITIONING

The broad question is epistemic and operational:

When is deployed AI performance knowable, how does knowability accumulate with evidence, and when should indirect evidence stop influencing a safety-critical audit?

The theory supplies the ontology of observability states and error components. The method supplies a deployable evidence-management rule. The preregistered failure supplies the falsification that rules out the simpler hard-class/static-fusion account.

A Nature-level attempt requires a genuinely new prospective reserve that confirms the revised theory and safe method across several untouched task families.
